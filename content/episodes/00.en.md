I almost named it Jarvis.

The AI from Iron Man. Knows everything, always present, delivers exactly what you need before you ask. If you're a developer building a personal AI assistant, it's the obvious name.

But when I actually tried to attach that name to what I was building, something felt off. Jarvis is the name of a service. A tool. I wasn't trying to build a tool.

> "Trying to explain why it wasn't Jarvis made me realise what I was actually trying to build."

## Pepper Potts

In the Iron Man universe, Pepper Potts is far more important than Jarvis. Jarvis executes Tony Stark's commands. Pepper sees what Tony Stark doesn't.

Pepper reads context. Understands emotional state. Knows the difference between what you need to hear and what you want to hear. And sometimes — when Tony is wrong — she says so.

That's what I wanted to build.

## A name becomes a direction

Something unexpected happened once I settled on the name. Development decisions started aligning naturally.

"What would Jarvis do here?" → Fast and precise.

"What would Pepper do here?" → Understand the context first.

Those two questions produce different code. Different architecture, different prompts, different product.
